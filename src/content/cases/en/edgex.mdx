## Overview

On June 1, 2026, EDGE — the native token of **edgeX**, an Amber Group-incubated perpetual DEX — collapsed roughly 77% in about an hour, from $1.26 to $0.315. Market cap fell from over $440M the day prior to $215.83M, 24h liquidations ran ~$6.2M, and more than 3,840 traders were affected.

What sets this case apart from RAVE and LAB is the **direction and the accused**. Those were *pumps*, and analysts alleged manipulation *by the team*. EDGE was a *crash*, and the **team itself claimed to be the victim** of external manipulation. Three explanations of the same event collide.

> "This was not a hack, exploit, or security breach. What we have identified so far suggests deliberate attempts by an external party to manipulate the market price of EDGE." — edgeX official statement (quoted)

> "We all know edgeX supply was being controlled by a few insiders with a low float." — ZachXBT (quoted)

Who is right is hard to settle after the fact. Exchange preliminary reviews leaned toward "thin liquidity," and neither edgeX's manipulation claim nor the exchanges' no-fault conclusion has been independently verified. So the value of this case is not in *naming a culprit* but in this: **every structural risk signal was already visible *before* the crash** — a low circulating ratio, round-number whale wallets, derivatives crowding. We measured the supply concentration ourselves by indexing the contract directly.

EDGE is an ERC-20 on Ethereum mainnet (`0xB007…a241`), unrelated to the similarly named "Edge Network" or "EDGE-X" tokens.

## Supply structure

### Market structure — how it differs from RAVE/LAB

| Exchange | Spot | Perpetual |
|----------|------|-----------|
| **Binance** | ❌ | O (pre-market) |
| OKX | O | O |
| Bybit | — | O |
| Coinbase | O | — |
| Kraken | O | — |
| Bitget | O | O |
| Gate.io | O | O |
| edgeX (own DEX) | — | O (native) |

There is an important difference. For RAVE and LAB, the core flaw was "no major spot anywhere." EDGE, by contrast, **does have major spot** on OKX, Coinbase, Kraken, Bitget and Gate. The one missing is *Binance* spot. So reading this as a simple `alpha-no-spot` case would be misleading.

The real issue is **shallow aggregate depth relative to the number of listings**. The market is fragmented across many exchanges plus edgeX's own perp, and derivatives (OI, volume) ran far ahead of spot depth. The "thin liquidity" the exchanges pointed to is exactly this structure — shake a thin pocket on one venue and the mark price on several exchanges' perps moves together.

### Token distribution

1B total supply, ~350M circulating (35%), **MC/FDV ≈ 0.35** (FDV ≈ 2.86× market cap) — a low-float, high-FDV structure. The published tokenomics are roughly:

| Tranche | Share | Lockup |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Genesis airdrop (early users) | 25% | unlocked at TGE |
| Pre-TGE Season | up to 5% | TGE +24h |
| Future Reserve | 30% | 18-mo lock, then linear |
| Core Contributors | 25% | 24-mo lock, then linear |
| Ecosystem & Community | 5% | 12-mo cliff |
| Foundation | 5% | 12-mo cliff |
| Liquidity | 5% | for market liquidity |

So roughly 65% of supply was locked or reserved from day one. Arkham went further, finding on-chain that developer-related wallets held ~69.5%, partners/LPs 14%, and only ~9.5% was actually circulating.

### Supply distribution — our own measurement

We indexed every transfer of the Ethereum contract `0xB007…a241` to compute net balances per wallet (as of 2026-06-12; 13,559 wallets with a positive balance, summing to ≈ the full supply), then double-checked the top wallets' identities via `ethereum.creation_traces` and `cex.addresses`.

- Wallets #1, 2, 4, 5, 7 (300M · 250M · 50M · 50M · 30.8M = **68.1% combined**) are all **contracts deployed by the same address `0x4e1d…ec67` within ~10 minutes on 2026-03-25** (6 days pre-TGE). The round numbers aren't coincidence — they are the published Future Reserve (30%), Core Contributors (25%) and 5% tranche lockups.
- The top 2 = **55.00%**, the top 5 (excluding burn) = **70.53%**
- Rank 8 is a **Bybit hot wallet ("Bybit 5")** and rank 32 a **Bitget hot wallet ("Bitget 35")** — both confirmed via `cex.addresses`. Rank 32 is the very address seen in the RAVE and LAB cases.
- Rank 6 is the burn address (`0x…dead`) — ~4% (40M) permanently burned
- The genuinely dispersed float thins out fast below that: under 1% from rank 9

Beyond the size match, the contract-deployment fact confirms it: our measurement (top contract cluster = 68.1%) points exactly the same way as Arkham's "~65% locked / ~9.5% actually circulating."

## Method analysis — three conflicting explanations

What makes this crash interesting is not *what happened* but that the parties flatly disagree on *why*.

### 1. The edgeX team — "victim of external manipulation"

The team framed the crash as a deliberate external price-manipulation attempt, said the protocol was never compromised, and denied selling its own allocations. It then offered a 200,000 USDC bounty to identify the attackers and a refund plan for liquidated longs (capped at 100,000 USDC per user).

### 2. ZachXBT — "low float, insider-controlled"

ZachXBT was skeptical of the "victim" frame. He argued EDGE's supply was controlled by a few insiders on a low float and demanded the team "name the counterparties / MM agreements which lead to these events." When the exchanges produced no-fault preliminary reviews, he shot back:

> "We investigated ourselves and did not find ourselves guilty even though we control nearly the entire supply." — ZachXBT (quoted)

He did not name any specific individual or market maker.

### 3. Exchange preliminary reviews — "thin liquidity"

Per what edgeX relayed, preliminary reviews from OKX, Bybit, Bitget and Bithumb pointed to thin liquidity and a liquidation cascade rather than organized manipulation. One outlet, however, noted the exchange messages were partly redacted and could not be independently verified, and no exchange issued a first-party statement directly (all came through edgeX).

### givenoxbt's allegation — and our own on-chain check

On-chain analyst givenoxbt (a niche account) said there were simultaneous withdrawals across Bybit, OKX, Bitget and Gate around the crash, and ~$1.6M pulled from edgeX's SpotVault — a "classic market maker farewell move." We tested this "premeditated dump" frame directly on-chain, measuring EDGE's exchange deposits and withdrawals on Ethereum L1 day by day. The answer doesn't tilt cleanly one way.

**1) The lockup contracts never moved.** The five lockup contracts identified above (68.1%) sent not a single token to an exchange during the crash window. The team's "we did not sell our allocations" claim is consistent with the on-chain record.

**2) There was no pre-crash loading of exchanges.** Sell-side deposits into exchanges (Inflow) were flat through May 31 at 130K–580K EDGE/day. Then on the crash day (6/1) they jumped to ~**3.98M**, and the next day (6/2) to ~**9.65M EDGE** — a **~25x surge**. The deposit spike didn't *precede* the crash; it broke out *with and after* it. That's the opposite of the "large exchange deposits right before the pump" pattern seen in RAVE and LAB.

**3) Not a single dumper, but a few large sellers + a long tail.** The biggest depositors on 6/1–2 were one EOA sending 2.5M EDGE to Bybit, another ~1.64M to Bitget, another 1M to OKX. The top 3 are ~38%, but the rest is spread across hundreds of addresses (890 + 357 transfers over two days), none of them lockup contracts. And **exchange withdrawals (Outflow) spiked the same days** (6/1 ~6.64M, 6/2 ~7.82M EDGE) — the flow was bidirectional.

**4) A familiar address.** That rank-32 Bitget hot wallet (`0xffa8db7b…cd54`) is the same "Bitget 35" from the RAVE and LAB cases — the same exchange infrastructure recurring across cases.

Taken together, the on-chain ERC-20 flows fit **"thin liquidity + a liquidation/panic cascade" better than "an external party pre-loaded exchanges for a premeditated dump"** — there were a few large sellers, but no sign of one-sided pre-positioning by a single entity. The caveat: CEX-internal order books, edgeX's own perp/SpotVault, and off-chain market-making are invisible on-chain, so this measurement alone can't fully settle whether manipulation occurred. What we can say is: as far as the chain shows, the evidence for a premeditated dump is weak.

### Why it was structurally possible

Whichever explanation holds, the premise is the same: **a small free float, price discovery concentrated in derivatives, and thin spot depth.** Such a token can have a thin pocket on one venue broken with little capital, and the shaken mark price then cascade-liquidates long positions across several exchanges' perps. edgeX's own post-mortem put the large-trader long share at 68.2% just before the collapse — a state primed for a cascade. Manipulation or plain thin liquidity, the mechanism that drags price down is identical.

## Lessons — signals to spot the next one

EDGE is not a case with an obvious culprit. That is precisely why the lesson is clear. Whether it was manipulation or thin liquidity is disputed even after the fact — but **every structural risk signal was observable before the crash.** A single item is weak; several overlapping ones are a strong warning.

### Supply-structure signals

- Circulating ratio under 35% of total supply (MC/FDV ≈ 0.35) — low-float, high-FDV
- A few wallets holding large round numbers (EDGE: top 2 = 55%, top 5 = 70%+)
- A large locked/reserved share whose terms can't be ruled out as post-hoc changeable (EDGE: upgradeable ERC1967Proxy — check whether admin is renounced)
- Analyst findings that the airdrop concentrated into clusters of fresh / linked wallets

### Market / derivatives signals

- Many listings but thin per-venue spot depth (listing count ≠ liquidity)
- Open interest excessive relative to TVL (EDGE: $1B+ OI alleged against ~$200M TVL)
- Long/short skewed ~70% to one side (EDGE: 68.2% long just before the crash)
- Trading volume abnormally large without narrative or hype

### On-chain / governance signals

- An upgradeable proxy token contract (extra risk if admin/timelock status is unverified)
- Pre-launch community channels closed off (EDGE: TGE post comments disabled, limiting vesting/MM questions)
- Exchange/intermediary addresses from prior suspected cases reappearing (EDGE: RAVE/LAB's Bitget hot wallet shows up in EDGE)

### How to read the post-crash narrative

- When a team frames a crash as "external manipulation," look first at **where supply control sits.** A "victim" claim from an entity controlling nearly all the supply is hard to take at face value before verification.
- "The exchanges investigated and found nothing" — check **who said it, in a verifiable form** (EDGE: no first-party exchange statement; messages partly redacted).
- Whether the claim is "external dump" or "insider pre-positioning," **check the on-chain exchange-deposit timing yourself.** Did supply pile into exchanges *before* the crash, or cascade in *during/after* it? (EDGE: the latter — deposits surged 25x starting *on* the crash day, with weak signs of pre-loading.)
- Refunds and bounties can be trust-rebuilding signals, but they do not substitute for establishing the cause.

> A token with concentrated supply and derivatives crowding is dangerous to position in either direction — pump or crash. The safest choice is **not to trade it.**
