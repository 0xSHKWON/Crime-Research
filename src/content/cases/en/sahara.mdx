## Overview

Sahara AI is a decentralized AI blockchain backed by a $43M round from Pantera, Binance Labs, and Polychain. SAHARA listed on Binance and Upbit in June 2025 and peaked at an all-time high of $0.1605 a month later, on July 24, 2025. It then gave back ~90% over the following year, and on June 9, 2026 fell a further ~55–60% in a single day ($0.0396 → $0.0145), liquidating roughly $22.65M of long positions.

This case reads differently from the others on this site. Unlike RAVE and LAB — which came with a ZachXBT-grade exposé and exchange investigations — SAHARA has (1) no such analyst exposé, (2) no exchange investigation (only the team's own internal one), and (3) a specific, falsifiable rebuttal from the team: the large flagged transfer was not an insider dump but a **liquidity fill into a Chainlink CCIP cross-chain bridge**. And a nearly identical single-day crash had already happened on November 29, 2025 — explained then as an unnamed market maker unwinding.

So we do not write this page as an assertion of manipulation. Instead it covers two things — **how to read an ambiguous large on-chain transfer**, and **what structural conditions made this token repeatedly crash-prone**. The one question that matters most (where did the June 9 transfer actually go?) we verified ourselves by indexing the Ethereum contract.

> "No team or investor tokens have been sold or moved."
> — Sahara AI (June 9 rebuttal). On the other side, analysts alleged an "insider dump ahead of the unlock." This page asserts neither, and measures only what is verifiable on-chain.

The first thing to note is that SAHARA **has a full Binance spot listing**. It is *not* the "no Binance spot, only Alpha/perp" structure that defined RAVE and LAB. It is a well-funded project with two major spot venues (Binance, Upbit) and deep order books. And yet it fell ~90% in a year and crashed ~60% in a single day twice — so the risk here lives not in the listing structure but in the structure of *supply, leverage, and demand*.

### Allocation and unlock overhang

| Item | Value |
|------|-------|
| Total / max supply | 10,000,000,000 SAHARA |
| Circulating (Jun 2026) | ~3.41B (34.07%) |
| Locked | 65.93% (cliff mechanism, vesting into 2029) |
| Ecosystem Development | 33.93% |
| Community Incentives | 20.75% |
| Early Backers | 19.75% |
| Core Team & Contributors | 15.00% |
| Airdrops / Buidlpad / Liquidity | 8.15% / 1.42% / 1.00% |
| Token contract (Ethereum) | `0xFDFfB411C4A70AA7C95D5C981a6Fb4Da867e1111` |
| FDV / market cap (Jun 2026) | ~$161M / ~$55M |

Two things define the structure. First, only 34% circulates and **66% is locked** — released on cliffs. The first major insider unlock (Core Team 15% + Early Backers 19.75% = 34.75%) falls around June 2026, ~12 months after TGE, and both crashes happened on the approach to that unlock cliff. Second, FDV is $161M against a $55M market cap — an MC/FDV of 0.34. A large un-circulating supply hangs over a thin float: a textbook low-float overhang.

That said, this is not RAVE-style concentration where "a few wallets hold 90% of the float." Most insider supply sits locked in vesting contracts. The nature of the risk is not *present wallet concentration* but *pre-announced supply growth against a thin real float*. The distinction matters.

## Method Analysis — "dump or bridge?"

The June 9 dispute reduces to a single question. Analysts read the ~600M SAHARA transfer (and "500M to Upbit") as evidence of an insider dump; the team called the same transfer a liquidity fill into its Chainlink CCIP bridge pool. Which one it is *is not a matter of opinion — it is verifiable on-chain*. We indexed transfers of the Ethereum token contract (`0xFDFf…1111`) and traced the source and destination of the large June 7–11 flows ourselves.

The measurement leans toward the team's account. The largest flow in the window is a 2-hop relay, `0xb9d9…9c66` → `0xd3b8…0f22` → `0x194e…62b4`, moving ~900M in 150M/300M chunks ~2 minutes apart per hop. The **destination `0x194e` is a contract, not an EOA**, deployed on 2026-06-02 — 7 days before the crash. Exchange deposit addresses, Upbit's included, are EOAs — so this 900M was *not* "dumped to Upbit." The sources `0xb9d9` and `0xd3b8` are also contracts, and `0xb9d9` was created by the same deployer around SAHARA's TGE (2025-06-25), consistent with Sahara-operated infrastructure.

That `0x194e` is not a static holding wallet but **cycles hundreds of millions of tokens per day, locking and releasing**, also fits bridge-pool behavior. And its outflows scatter to *dozens of distinct non-CEX wallets* at 14–52M each — what a CCIP LockReleaseTokenPool looks like when it releases tokens to users withdrawing cross-chain.

**Cross-chain check.** To close the "maybe the Upbit flow was on another chain" objection, we indexed BNB Chain the same way. On *both* chains the same relay contracts (`0x194e`, `0xd3b8`, `0xb9d9`) moved tokens in 150M chunks — but in **opposite directions**: *into* the pool on Ethereum, *out of* it on BNB. That is one operator holding the same contracts on two chains and shuffling inventory between them — textbook cross-chain bridge behavior. And **no Upbit-labeled flow appears on either chain.** The largest BNB-side exchange flows were also Binance-internal moves, the `0x26209`↔Bitget 6 round-trip, and many small distributed deposits into Binance (58, 70, 41, 35 transfers into one hot wallet).

We note the limits honestly. (1) `cex.addresses`' Upbit deposit labels may be incomplete — but on neither chain is there a *single ~500M block* transfer to one exchange deposit address (the largest CEX flows are Binance-internal, the Bitget round-trip, or small distributed deposits). (2) We did not assert at the ABI level that `0x194e` is literally Chainlink's CCIP contract (Dune has not decoded it); we say only that its deploy timing and two-chain cycling are *consistent* with that explanation. The bottom line: the specific "team dumped 500M to Upbit" claim is not reproduced on **either** Ethereum or BNB Chain, and the transfer pattern fits the bridge account.

So where did the selling that actually pushed price down on June 9 come from? On-chain, the flows toward exchanges were not one insider's bulk deposit but **many distinct wallets making small, distributed deposits into Binance** (147, 38, and 40 transfers into a single hot wallet — and the same pattern on BNB: 58, 70, 41 transfers). In other words, on top of a setup of unlock cliff + heavy leverage + thin real demand, *many holders panic-sold at once*. We also record that the Bitget hot wallet `0x1ab4…8f23` (Bitget 6) — the same address seen in the RAVE and LAB cases — reappears with ~350M tokens of throughput in the same window: the same exchange infrastructure recurring across cases.

## Lessons — signals to identify the next case like this

The takeaway here differs from RAVE/LAB. It is not about asserting "who dumped," but about **how to verify an ambiguous signal, and what structure repeatedly invites a crash**.

### On-chain verification signals (this case's distinction)

- For a large transfer, first check **whether the destination is a contract or an EOA** — exchange deposits are EOAs; bridge/staking pools are contracts
- Check whether the destination carries an **exchange deposit label** (Etherscan/Dune)
- Check whether that address **holds or forwards** the tokens — a pool that cycles may be bridge/MM behavior
- A tweet saying "500M moved to X" is held until you *check the destination yourself* — don't promote an allegation into a fact

### Supply / unlock signals

- When circulating is ~30% of supply and **the rest is locked on cliffs**, that is pre-announced overhang
- The closer the first major insider (team + backers) unlock, the more sell-pressure expectation is priced in
- A low MC/FDV (0.34 here) means the market is thin relative to un-circulating supply

### Derivatives / leverage signals

- When a single-day crash comes with **large long liquidations** ($22.65M), it is a leverage cascade
- Low float plus high perp open interest is an environment where small selling triggers liquidation chains

### Recurrence signal (most important)

- The same token taking **two or more similar single-day crashes in a short span** (~60% each in Nov 2025 and Jun 2026 here)
- Each explained by an *external factor* (market-maker unwind → bridge fill), with the explanation changing each time
- Whether or not each explanation is true, *the fact that it is repeatedly crash-prone* is itself the warning

### Trading takeaway

> A structurally crash-prone token is dangerous regardless of whether the allegation is true.

When low float, an imminent unlock, high leverage, and single-market-maker dependence stack up — whether insiders actually dumped or it was a bridge fill — a position in either direction is dangerous. An allegation being *unproven* does not mean the token is *safe*.
