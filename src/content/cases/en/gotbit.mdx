## Overview

The protagonist of this case is not a token that crashed. It is a token that **was never real to begin with**. NexFundAI (NEXF) was a trap token the FBI created for a market-manipulation investigation — an ERC-20 purporting to represent shares in an AI-related fund. Posing as the project's operators, agents approached market makers with a simple ask — "make the token look actively traded" — and collected the sales pitches, contracts and bot activity of the firms that took the job.

On October 9, 2024, the U.S. Attorney's Office for the District of Massachusetts announced charges against 18 individuals and entities — the first time the DOJ criminally charged crypto "financial services firms" (market makers) with market manipulation and wash trading. Four market makers appear in the filings: Gotbit, ZM Quant, CLS Global and MyTrade, alongside executives of client token projects (including Saitama, at one point a multi-billion-dollar market cap, and Robo Inu). More than $25M in crypto was seized, and trading bots running across roughly 60 tokens were deactivated.

> "purportedly offering market making services … in fact engaged in artificial trading of digital assets to give the false appearance that there was an active (and heavily traded) market"
> — the filings' description of the scheme. Market making on the label, artificial trading underneath — that is the prosecution's account, and several defendants, including Gotbit, later admitted it in court.

Where our other cases run "allegation → verification," this one runs the other way. **The court record already documented the method** — so we measured the footprint that method left on-chain. Indexing the contract the community attributes to NexFundAI (`0x16ca…54c` — the filings don't name an address, so we label it "attributed"), we found: 12 dormant weeks after deploy → four weeks of mechanical churn by 15–19 wallets → a dead stop three weeks before the charges were unsealed. Few specimens show what wash trading looks like on-chain this cleanly.

## Market structure — a market with no market

Conventional supply analysis doesn't apply to NEXF: it was never a token sold to investors, it was an investigative instrument. Instead, its trading structure is the essence of the case — **a market whose participants were essentially all bots**.

| Item | Value (own measurement) |
|------|------|
| Chain / venue | Ethereum / single Uniswap pool |
| Attributed contract | `0x16ca471aE755f8a2cD4eC315A4a7439dcfEBE54c` |
| Full lifetime | 2024-05-29 to 09-18 (112 days) |
| Lifetime on-chain transfers | 2,600 |
| Lifetime DEX trades / takers | 2,186 / 652 (mostly deploy-day) |
| Bot-window (08-22–09-18) trades / takers | 827 / **36** |
| Bot-window "volume" | ~$607K (avg trade $734) |

For four weeks, this token's entire "market" was 36 takers. Those 36 printed $20–30K a day — $607K in total. From the outside, the chart would have read as "a small token with steady turnover."

## Method — manipulation-as-a-service

What this case documents is not one token's misbehavior but an **industrialized service product**. Per the filings, the market makers pitched "market making," then sold a product that repeatedly traded tokens between wallets they controlled to manufacture volume. ZM Quant advertised a trading bot that could "create volume"; sales happened over Telegram and video calls, the filings state. Client projects were charged with using that manufactured activity to draw investors in and then selling into the artificially supported prices — the classic pump-and-dump structure.

### The on-chain fingerprint of wash trading — buys ≈ sells

Aggregate the bot window's top wallets and the fingerprint is unmistakable. Excluding the pool, every top wallet **sent almost exactly as much as it received**:

| Wallet | sends | recvs | Sent | Received | Gap |
|--------|-------|-------|------|----------|-----|
| `0x19e7…1d7a` (Uniswap pool) | 404 | 425 | 516.7B | 518.1B | — |
| `0x7699…444e` | 40 | 39 | 50,802,652,773 | 50,802,358,640 | **0.0006%** |
| `0xcc98…ccfc` | 31 | 36 | 64,208,172,129 | 64,207,356,260 | 0.001% |
| `0x2ae4…727b` | 32 | 33 | 56.77B | 56.77B | ≈0 |
| `0xc9e7…8fe1` | 29 | 31 | 56.19B | 56.19B | ≈0 |
| 14 further similar wallets | 17–29 | 17–31 | ≈ | ≈ | ≈0 |

Buy what you sold, sell what you bought. Net position change over four weeks: effectively zero — no price exposure, no profit-seeking. The sole output of this trading is **the volume itself**. That symmetry cannot arise from organic demand, and it is the on-chain form of the "wash trading between controlled wallets" the filings describe.

## Lessons — volume is a purchasable product

The lesson here is simple and heavy: **trading volume is not a trust signal.** The court record confirms that volume-manufacturing was a service genuinely for sale, and that a few dozen bots complete the look of an "actively traded token."

### Signals for checking the quality of volume

- **Trades vs unique traders** — NEXF's bot window: 827 trades, 36 takers. When participants are abnormally few relative to volume, question where the volume comes from
- **Per-wallet cumulative buys ≈ sells** — if the most active wallets sell exactly what they buy (no net position change), that volume is churn, not demand
- **Metronomic uniformity** — daily trade counts and volume holding a narrow band for weeks looks more like bot output than a human market
- **Sudden 'activation' after long dormancy** — when a token with no organic demand starts trading on a steady rhythm one day, ask who signed what at that moment

### Structural takeaways

- **DEX volume is at least verifiable** — this case's fingerprint (taker counts, wallet symmetry) was countable because it was on-chain. CEX internal volume allows no such verification — treat the reported volume of thin CEX-listed tokens with one more level of doubt
- **Listing requirements create manipulation demand** — because exchanges weigh volume in listing and maintenance reviews, projects have a structural incentive to buy it. Impressive volume right after a new listing proves nothing by itself
- **Market-maker contracts are a black box** — when a project doesn't disclose which MM it engaged and on what terms, investors have no way to judge the authenticity of its volume (part of why Binance mandated MM disclosure in March 2026)
- **The method did not die with the indictments** — as of 2026, the industry's own assessment is that wash trading remains "far more common" than expected among low-cap tokens. That is why the NEXF fingerprint is worth memorizing

> A chart's volume bars don't show how many people care. They may show how much someone paid to make it look that way.
