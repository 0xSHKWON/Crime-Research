## Overview

AriaAI (ARIA) is **the only case to suffer two ~91% crashes within nine days**. After a first crash of -83% on April 9, 2026 (a leverage unwind), the token rallied +700% to a new all-time high of $1.18 — and just six days later, in the early hours of April 15, it collapsed -91% again. EmberCN (Yu Jin)'s on-chain analysis reports that eight wallets had withdrawn 45.64M ARIA from Gate.io three weeks before the crash, kept the balances dormant, then sold in coordination during the small hours to cash out $5.42M USDT. Market cap evaporated from $315M to $38.5M in four hours.

This page cites EmberCN's *"8 wallets = single entity"* reading directly but does not assert it as fact. The core message of the case is that ARIA's *genuine fundamentals* (a real game in Tower Rank, real users, $5M institutional funding) and the manipulation setup *coexisted*.

EmberCN's trace was **independently verified through our own Dune indexing** (ARIA BSC contract activity reproduced via `transfers_bnb.erc20`). Reproducing EmberCN's published 8-wallet table — addresses, sell volumes, USDT received — our indexing finds that **all 8 wallets received ARIA from a single Gate.io hot wallet `0x0d07…2fe`**, and that the totals (45.65M ARIA sold, $5.43M USDT received) agree with EmberCN's figures *to five decimal places*. Detailed measurements are written up in the "Independently verified through our own indexing" section under *Supply Structure*.

> "Another 'meme coin' ate well and then dumped. Suspected $ARIA manipulators dumped 45.64M ARIA for 5.42M USDT, 'crashing' the $ARIA price by 91%."
> — @alphatorisk (paraphrasing EmberCN's analysis)

> "These 8 wallets are almost certainly under the control of a single entity. They appear separate on the surface, but the synchronized trading pattern, the identical withdrawal source, and the identical sell band strongly suggest otherwise."

## Supply Structure — EmberCN's 8-wallet trace

The core piece of evidence in this case is the precise on-chain analysis from EmberCN (Yu Jin).

### Stage 1 — Pre-staging (around 2026-03-25, three weeks before the crash)

Eight wallets **withdrew 45.64M ARIA from Gate.io**. The timing is decisive:
- The withdrawal coincides with *when ARIA's price first began rising*
- That is, *as the pump was getting started*, insiders were already moving the tokens into tradeable form
- Exchange → personal wallet = a "ready to sell" signal

### Stage 2 — Pump (early to mid-April)

Price climbed steadily. After the first crash on April 9, a +700% rebound carried it to a new ATH. Through this period, **the 8 wallets did not move** — accumulation / dormant phase.

### Stage 3 — Dump (early hours of April 15)

The 8 wallets sold *simultaneously*:
- Total **45,640,000 ARIA**
- Average sell price **$0.12** (down -90% from the peak of $1.18, but profitable because the average cost was much lower)
- Cash-out: **$5.42M USDT**
- Price collapsed from $1.01 → $0.09 within four hours

### EmberCN's conclusion (quoted)

> "These 8 wallets are almost certainly under the control of a single entity. They appear separate on the surface, but the synchronized trading pattern, the identical withdrawal source, and the identical sell band strongly suggest otherwise."

This page cites EmberCN's reading as-is, but is explicit that the *8 wallets = single entity* claim is the kind of inference that calls for additional forensic work (gas-funder graph, fund-merge points, full cluster graph).

### First crash — primarily leverage unwind (partially adjusted by our own measurement)

The first crash on 4/9 reads *primarily* as a **leverage unwind**:
- Futures volume $1.49B vs spot $29M (51:1)
- Open Interest $83.1M, liquidations $9.4M
- 6 wallets stayed dormant after their withdrawals
- The crash itself was driven by the *market's own leverage structure*

That said, our Dune indexing also catches **2 of the 8 wallets** (`0xf4ad…5fc5`, `0xd732…4ba9`) starting incremental selling **from 2026-04-09 10:33 ~ 10:35 UTC** — splitting roughly **9.46M ARIA** across six days through **2,324 swaps**. Whether this selling was the *trigger* of the leverage cascade or a *reaction* to it requires minute-level swap-event analysis, and this page does not assert which.

→ In other words, the first crash reads less as *pure* leverage unwind and more as **leverage unwind + some insider selling** combined. The genuine *real distribution* only begins with the second crash (04-14 16:55 UTC), when all 8 wallets become active.

### Recovery phase — setting up exit liquidity

What the +700% rebound implies:
- The retail liquidated in the first crash is replaced by *fresh retail* coming in to "buy the bottom"
- The "this time it's really the bottom" trap
- That fresh retail becomes the *exit liquidity* for the 8 wallets

### Second crash — real distribution (8 wallets selling in unison)

A fundamentally different kind of crash from the first:
- Selling in the small hours of UTC (when Asian retail is asleep or US trading has just closed)
- 8 wallets acting *at the same time* — algorithmic or coordinated
- Split sells (not a single dump) — minimizing slippage and diluting identity
- Average $0.12 sell price — down -90% from the peak, but profitable thanks to a much lower insider cost basis

### What "twice in nine days" means

| First (4/9) | Second (4/15) |
|----------|-----------|
| Leverage unwind-led | Real distribution |
| Retail leverage liquidation | 8 wallets finishing their selling |
| 6 wallets dormant + 2 wallets begin incremental selling | All 8 wallets active |
| -83% / 1 hour | -91% / 4 hours |
| Recoverable | Technical structure broken |

→ Retail can hardly tell that the first crash is not *the* real sell signal. Retail that survived the first crash absorbs the *actual* loss in the second.

## Lessons — Signals to identify similar cases

ARIA's core message: *"a token that has crashed once can crash again"* + *"genuine fundamentals and manipulation can coexist"*.

### Pre-staging signals (the strongest)

- **Large exchange withdrawals occurring *right before* a pump begins** — insiders setting up to sell
- **Withdrawn balances staying *dormant for weeks*** — held in ready-to-sell form
- **Multiple wallets withdrawing from the *same exchange in a similar window*** — likely a single entity's split activity

### Signals of a possible second crash

- **A rapid V-shaped rebound, like +500%, after a first crash** — when retail was liquidated but insiders did not yet dump, insiders are still holding
- **The first crash being purely *leverage unwind*** — without actual selling, insiders remain in position
- **A new ATH being printed** — gives insiders a *real distribution* opportunity
- **Unresolved audit concerns + rising price** — the market actively ignoring risk

### Late-night selling pattern

- **Trading in the small hours of UTC (Korea morning hours)** — when Asian retail is asleep or US trading has just closed
- **Multiple wallets acting *simultaneously*** — algorithmic or coordinated
- **Split sells (not a single dump)** — to minimize slippage and dilute identity
