## Overview

SIREN is a rare case in which **the same pump-and-dump cycle repeated three times** over a single month in March 2026. Market cap roughly doubled three times and fully retraced three times, with fresh retail flowing in on each leg. A triple disclosure from ZachXBT, Bubblemaps, and EmberCN landed in the same window right before the first crash — and yet the exchanges did not intervene.

ZachXBT reported that the 48.5% cluster wallets showed on-chain connections to DWF Labs-linked tokens, while DWF Labs has consistently denied the related allegations. **This page does not assert that DWF Labs engaged in manipulation, and explicitly notes that on-chain connections are circumstantial evidence — not direct proof.**

> "One entity controls ~50% of SIREN. One cluster holds over $1B. This only ends one way."
> — Bubblemaps (warning posted hours before the first crash)

### 484M distributed in 24 hours — in-house indexing

This page independently indexed the transfer events of the SIREN contract (`0x997A…18e1`) via Dune (based on `transfers_bnb.erc20`). The findings are as follows.

- **484,103,975 SIREN** (~**48%** of supply, assuming a 1B total) was accumulated into the single distribution contract `0x2aa5…a351` between **2026-02-05 and 02-06 (a 24-hour window)** — 905 of 906 receives (**99.99%**) came from a single source
- The single source is another contract, `0xb1c5…6699` — **905 batched transfers within 24 hours**
- The same 484M was then distributed almost entirely between **2026-03-21 10:19 and 03-22 11:53 UTC (roughly 25 hours)** via 905 transfers
- The final unlock transfer landed at **2026-03-22 11:53 UTC** — about 30 minutes before the first ATH
- Distribution to the top 30 receivers was concentrated between **2026-03-22 09:13 and 10:56 UTC (1 hour 43 minutes)**. Each received 2–5M SIREN, totaling roughly 96M (~20% of the 484M) — the remaining 80% went to smaller wallets within the same 25-hour window
- Both contracts were **deployed in 2022** as reusable infrastructure (with a track record of handling ETH, BUSD, USDC, CAKE, and other tokens) — presumed to be a Hedgey- or Sablier-style vesting / claim contract

This analysis backs EmberCN's *"~$1B withdrawn from vesting"* circumstantial claim with directly measurable facts. That said, **which specific vesting service the contract is an instance of** cannot be asserted without BscScan source-code verification.

### Stage 1 — Fundamental setup

SIREN entered an effectively abandoned state shortly after launch. With no real users or product, the token survived on *narrative plus market-maker capital* alone. In tokens like this, most of the volume is likely wash trading between insider wallets, which keeps the accumulation cost far below the market price.

### Stage 2 — Pre-positioning

According to EmberCN's analysis, 48 of the cluster wallets accumulated *around* 2026-03-22, while the remaining 4 had been pre-positioned since June and July 2025. The average accumulation cost is around $0.045 — effectively an unbounded profit zone at almost any exit price.

### Stage 3 — Short squeeze (same mechanism as RAVE)

While the pump was in progress, perps listed on Binance Futures and Bybit Futures, opening short baiting. In the same window, the cluster sent large deposits to exchanges → traders read this as a "dump imminent" signal → shorts accumulated → a sudden buy-side push triggered a liquidation cascade. At the peak, liquidations totaled $2.4M on Binance and $4.7M on Bybit, for a combined $7M+. Open Interest peaked at $105M.

### Stage 4 — The burn paradox

A 26% supply burn was executed, further shrinking the circulating float. When insiders already hold most of the supply, a burn **only makes manipulation easier**. A reminder that burns are not always bullish.

### Stage 5 — Repetition (the most sophisticated part)

First pump → first dump (realizing profit) → the same cluster accumulates again at a lower price → second pump → second dump → a third repetition. With each cycle, the average retail entry rises (losses on each dump) while the insider average entry falls.

## The DWF Labs connection — the most controversial area

This is the most sensitive part of the case. The source and the scope of the assertion need to be kept clearly separate.

### ZachXBT's finding (the factual portion)

On 2026-03-23, while graphing the 48.5% SIREN cluster wallets on BSC, ZachXBT reported finding that the addresses are on-chain connected to several obscure DWF-affiliated tokens (LADYS, RACA, TOMO, and others).

### Publicly available facts

- DWF Labs is a major crypto market maker
- DWF Labs has previously made a strategic investment in SIREN (public information)
- SIREN was supported under the BNB Chain Meme Liquidity Support Plan

The fact itself that *"DWF is SIREN's official market maker and an investor"* is publicly available information.

### DWF Labs' position

DWF Labs has denied the manipulation allegations around SIREN, and has consistently denied earlier allegations (LADYS and others).

### The scope of this page's assertion

**This page does not assert that DWF Labs engaged in manipulation.** ZachXBT's on-chain analysis only shows the fact that *the 48.5% cluster wallets exhibit on-chain connections to DWF-linked token wallets*. Whether that connection is direct evidence of manipulation or merely traces of normal market-making activity is something that cannot be concluded without off-chain materials (market-maker contracts, internal chat logs, and so on).

## Lessons — Signals to identify similar cases

SIREN is the case that most clearly illustrates the rule *"don't re-enter a token that has already crashed once."*

### Repeating-cycle signals

- A token that has already crashed once pumps to a similar level again within a short window — the first cycle may be a *prototype*, with the 2nd and 3rd as the real game
- The volume pattern in each cycle is nearly identical — a trace of algorithmic accumulate-and-dump
- Each cycle's peak steps progressively lower — artificial distribution

### Vesting / lockup-service signals

- A large unlock right *before* the pump from a vesting/claim contract like Hedgey or Sablier — locked tokens flipping into tradable state
- A gap of **less than 36 hours** between the vesting cliff and ATH is a very strong risk signal (SIREN: the final unlock landed 30 minutes before ATH)
- When 30%+ of supply sits in a single contract with an approaching vesting cliff, expect imminent volatility at the price-discovery venue (such as perps)
- When many of the receiving retail wallets had their **gas funded by exchange withdrawals** right after the unlock — not proof of a common entity, but consistent with preparation to sell through exchange channels

### Market-maker / VC connection signals

- An official market maker that is simultaneously a strategic investor — a dual conflict of interest
- Beneficiary of a chain-level incentive program (such as the BNB Chain Meme Liquidity Support) — capital and legitimacy secured in one shot
- Insider wallets sharing gas-funding or trading connections with other tokens of the same market maker — ZachXBT's forensic method

### Forensic-tool warnings

- A public warning from Bubblemaps — typically lands *right before* a crash
- A direct phrase like "This only ends one way" — a signal of analyst conviction

### The burn paradox

- A burn executed in a high-concentration setting — further compresses the float, making manipulation *easier*
- A reminder that burns are not always bullish

### Derivatives signals

- A short ratio above 50% during the pump — a squeeze setup in progress
- An abnormal OI / market-cap ratio — common to both RAVE and SIREN

### Listing-timing signals

- A near-dead token suddenly listing perps on a major exchange — expanding the price-discovery venue to maximize manipulation effect
- The pump starts within days of the listing — evidence of pre-setup

## Industry implications

1. **Forensic tools detect, but cannot stop.** Bubblemaps warned accurately *hours in advance* — yet without any exchange intervention, the pattern *repeated three times*. These tools only deliver value in combination with exchange willingness to act.
2. **Market maker = strategic investor — a structural conflict of interest.** Setting aside the truth of the DWF Labs allegations, the structure in which a single entity wears both market-maker and investor hats is an inherent conflict.
3. **Failure to learn through repetition.** Even with the same pattern repeating three times on the same token, fresh retail flowed in each round. Market information asymmetry remains extreme.
4. **On-chain alone is not 100% proof.** Even with strong circumstantial evidence, a definitive conclusion is hard to reach without off-chain materials such as market-maker contracts or internal logs.
